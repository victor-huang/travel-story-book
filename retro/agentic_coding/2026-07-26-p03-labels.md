# P03 — the labels arrive, and measure something unexpected (2026-07-26)

The first hand-labelled truth set: 175 photos over two real days. It was supposed to tell us
whether event detection hits ≥80%. It did — 27% F1 — but the more valuable output was two things
nobody was looking for.

---

### The labels found a nine-hour timestamp bug that every test had missed

**Category:** real data / seam

**Symptom.** Reviewing the labelling for format errors, the events looked *interleaved*: walking
forward in time, the event label switched 3 times for 3 events, where a contiguous labelling
switches exactly 2. My first read was that the human had grouped by theme instead of time.

**Root cause.** They hadn't. Two photos, `IMG_1881` and `IMG_1884`, were sitting nine hours from
their own filename neighbours:

```
IMG_1878  17:25     IMG_1883  17:26
IMG_1880  17:26     IMG_1884  08:27  ← wrong
IMG_1881  08:26  ←  IMG_1885  17:27
```

Camera filename sequence is monotonic, so those frames were shot seconds apart. The labeller had
put them with the afternoon because that is visibly where they belong. **The timestamps were wrong
and the labels were right.**

These are two of the seven offset-vs-GPS conflicts P01 found. The P02 amendment said "GPS wins any
disagreement", and T12 implemented that as *discard the tag and read the wall time as GPS-local*.
But the two signals answer different questions:

* the **offset tag** says which zone the camera's clock was reading — so it is the best evidence
  for the **UTC instant**, even when it is wrong about where you were;
* **GPS** says where the photo was taken — so it decides which zone to **display** in.

A phone still set to its home zone recorded `08:26 -07:00` in Vienna. The correct reading is
`08:26 -07:00` = `15:26Z` = **17:26 Vienna**. Discarding the tag gave 08:26 Vienna — nine hours out.

**Fix.** Take the instant from the tag, the zone from GPS, and re-render the local time. After it,
the sequence is monotonic with filename order and the labels are perfectly contiguous on both days
— which is itself the confirmation.

**Lesson.** **A human labelling for one purpose audits everything the labels touch.** Nobody set
out to check timestamps. The bug was invisible to every unit test (each stage was self-consistent),
invisible to the eval harness (it only scores boundaries), and invisible to me eyeballing contact
sheets. It took a person who knew what order the afternoon happened in.

Also: my first instinct was that the *human* had made the error. Worth noticing, because the
labels are the measurement instrument — treating an anomaly as instrument error rather than
signal is how you discard your best data.

---

### The stage overwrote its own input, so re-running degraded the answer

**Category:** design-flaw

**Symptom.** After fixing the logic, `--force timezones` changed nothing. The photos stayed at
08:26.

**Root cause.** T12 read the raw EXIF offset out of `tz_offset_minutes`/`tz_source` — the same two
columns it *writes* its resolved answer to. It had flagged this in its own docstring as a
workaround for the frozen `Media` model having no field for the raw tag. Once the stage ran, the
tag was gone: a re-run saw "no tag", took a different branch, and produced a worse answer than the
first run, with no error.

**Fix.** `media.exif_offset_minutes` as its own column (schema v3). Source data and derived data do
not share storage.

**Lesson.** **A stage that reads and writes the same field is not idempotent, and the failure is
silent and directional** — each run degrades. Worth checking anywhere a stage's input and output
overlap. The workaround was documented, which is why it was findable; the mistake was leaving it
in place once the schema was mine to change.

---

### The metric measured a definition mismatch, not a quality gap

**Category:** wrong-assumption

**Symptom.** Event boundaries: precision 18%, recall 50%, F1 27%, against a ≥80% target. A grid
search over gap/jump/duration could not exceed **F1 57%** anywhere.

**Root cause.** Look at where the boundaries actually are:

* **Human boundaries:** after a **2-minute** gap, and after an **8-minute** gap.
* **Pipeline boundaries:** at 17, 19, 23, 26, 45, 52, 57-minute gaps.

They are *anti-correlated*. The human is marking "we finished the museum and walked out" — which
happens with no pause at all, because you just walk out. The pipeline is splitting at every pause,
which the human considers *within* an event.

No amount of threshold tuning fixes that: a 2-minute-gap boundary is invisible to a gap-based rule.

This is precisely the **clusters vs chapters** distinction P02's reviewer raised, now measured
rather than asserted. What Module 6 produces is a time-and-location *cluster*; what a person
naturally labels is a narrative *chapter* (5 across 175 photos, versus the pipeline's 13).

Content change was the obvious candidate signal, and we already compute CLIP embeddings. Measured:
the three human boundaries sit at cosine distances 0.74 / 0.66 / 0.57 against a within-event median
of 0.32 — genuinely in the top tail — but **27 of 154 within-event pairs are at least as distant**.
Informative, not separable. Thresholding on it alone gives roughly 10% precision.

**Lesson.** **Before trusting a metric, check that both sides are measuring the same object.** The
acceptance criterion "≥80% against hand-labelled boundaries" silently assumed the human and the
algorithm mean the same thing by "event". They don't, and the number that results is a mismatch
score, not a quality score. Tuning against it would have been optimising toward the wrong target
with the appearance of rigour — the exact failure P02 was run to prevent, reappearing one layer
down.

Secondary: 4 true boundaries is too small a sample for a percentage to be meaningful anyway. Each
one is 25% of recall.

---

## Encoded as project rules

- `media.exif_offset_minutes` exists so resolution never overwrites its own input; schema v3.
- Timezone conflict resolution takes the *instant* from the tag and the *zone* from GPS, with the
  arithmetic and the real-data provenance in the module docstring and its tests.
- The clusters-vs-chapters mismatch is recorded in the tracker as an open question for the human,
  not silently resolved by re-tuning thresholds.
