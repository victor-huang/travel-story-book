# T24 — event detection, and a diagnosis that was wrong (2026-07-26)

Built Module 6. One entry, but it is the most uncomfortable one in this folder: **I wrote a
confident causal explanation into the plan doc, and measurement disproved it.**

---

### The P02 fix worked, for a reason I invented

**Cycle:** T24
**Cost:** none shipped — caught while building, because the code needed the mechanism to be real
**Category:** wrong-assumption

**Symptom.** None, which is the point. The P02 amendment was already in the plan doc, already
marked binding on T24, and already carried into a tracker row. It had produced a real improvement
on real data: a day that had collapsed into 4 events split into 7 plausible ones.

**Root cause.** Two changes were made together in the P02 prototype — comparing position against a
*recent window* of items instead of the whole event, and adding a *maximum event duration*. The
improvement was real; my attribution of it was not. I wrote a mechanism-level explanation into the
plan ("as an event accumulates items its centroid converges on the average of everything so far, so
individual 1–2 km moves stop exceeding the threshold") and never tested that the mechanism was the
one doing the work.

Measuring it while implementing:

| recent_window | max_minutes | events on the real day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6 | 150 | 7 |
| 12 | 150 | 7 |
| 1000 | 150 | 7 |
| 6 | ∞ | 4 |
| 1000 | ∞ | 4 |

The window makes **no difference at all**. `max_minutes` did every bit of the work.

Worse, a synthetic check showed the recent window is *actively harmful* for the case it was
supposed to fix. Under gradual drift the recent centroid **follows you**, so you are never far from
it and it never splits; a whole-event centroid lags behind the drift and does eventually notice. At
0.2 km per shot: whole-event centroid → 5 events, recent window → 1.

**Fix.** Removed `recent_window` entirely rather than shipping a knob that does nothing and reasons
backwards. Kept the whole-event centroid and `events.max_minutes`. The plan doc now records the
original claim *and* its disproof, rather than being quietly edited to look like it was always
right.

**Lesson.** **A fix that works is not evidence that your explanation of it works.** Two changes
landed together and I credited the interesting one. The explanation was plausible, mechanistic, and
completely wrong — and plausibility is exactly what makes that kind of error survive review, because
there is nothing to notice.

The specific habit worth keeping: when a change bundles two mechanisms, vary them independently
before writing down which one mattered. It cost one command here. Had `recent_window` shipped, it
would have been a permanent config knob with a confident docstring explaining a behaviour it does
not have — and the next person tuning event detection would have burned real time on it.

Secondary lesson: **the honest version of the finding is more useful than the tidy one.** What is
now in the plan — "wandering a city centre trips neither rule, only the clock catches it, and
whether `jump_km` should be smaller needs the labelled set" — is a sharper statement of the problem
than the original, and it names the open question instead of implying it was solved.

  the backstop exists to prevent.
