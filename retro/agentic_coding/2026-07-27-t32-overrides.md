# T32 overrides, and a hypothesis that took three tries to die (2026-07-27)

Feedback on the day-highlight sheets — 16 photos to add, 3 to drop — turned into a measurement
that killed the tuning approach outright, then T32.

---

### Three plausible hypotheses, each disproved by the sweep it suggested

**Category:** wrong-assumption
**Cost:** the good kind — an hour of measurement instead of a week of threshold-fiddling

**Symptom.** 19 hand-labelled decisions against a 31-photo selection.

**First hypothesis: the quota is too flat.** Day 18 held 132 photos, day 20 held 36, and both got
exactly 10 highlights. Obviously wrong, and 13 of the 16 adds were on day 18. Sweeping a
size-scaled quota `k·√n` recovered **4 of 16** at k=2.0 — while growing the book to 53.

**Second hypothesis: the picks are redundant in time.** Day 18's ten picks covered 11:03–21:59 but
clustered into five moments — four of them within 14 minutes — with a 4h39m hole where nine of the
adds live. All three drops were one half of a near-simultaneous pair (1, 11 and 0 minutes apart).
Adding a minimum time gap fixed the drops and still recovered only **4 of 16**.

**Third hypothesis: the diversity gate is broken.** It nearly is — CLIP cosine among chosen photos
runs 0.26–0.84 against a 0.85 gate, so it rejects 4 candidates across 277 photos. But the picks
already covered 10 distinct subjects. A full 3-D sweep over quota × time gap × diversity topped out
at **7 of 16**, at a 61-photo book.

**Root cause.** Quality does not rank what a person ranks. Adds mean 0.742 (0.643–0.799), drops
mean 0.713 (0.692–0.753), and 7 of 16 adds sit inside the drop range. Two subject-level signals
fail too: face *presence* is anti-correlated (18.8% of adds vs a 27.4% base rate) and a CLIP
landmark-vs-generic margin puts adds across the 25th–98th percentile against drops at 27th–71st.

**What actually found it: rendering the 19 photos on one sheet and looking.** They are one photo
each of sixteen named things — the Hofburg, St Stephen's, the Opera, the Musikverein, a Klimt. Two
of them rank 89th and 62nd of 121 within their event, because a couple standing in a busy plaza
scores badly on sharpness and contrast, and they are the two most obviously book-worthy photos
there.

**Lesson.** **Sweeping a parameter is how you find out the parameter is not the problem** — the
number to watch is the *ceiling* across the whole grid, not the best cell. 7 of 16 at double the
size is a refutation, not a tuning result. And this is the fourth time in this project that
rendering the output found in thirty seconds what a metric could not find at all. The eval harness
can rank options; it cannot notice that the options are all wrong.

### An implicit file lookup anchored to the current directory

**Category:** design-flaw
**Cost:** two failing tests, caught before commit

**Symptom.** `_overrides_path` fell back to `Path.cwd() / "overrides.toml"`. The moment I wrote a
real `overrides.toml` in the repo root to apply the feedback, two unrelated CLI tests failed:
the pytest process runs in the repo, so the tests picked it up and choked on filenames absent
from their fixture library.

**Root cause.** Convenience default anchored to *where the command runs* rather than *what it
operates on*. Failing tests were the benign symptom; the real one is that the same command means
different things from different directories, and a stray file in a checkout silently applies
itself to an unrelated trip.

**Fix.** The implicit lookup is anchored to the config file. With no `--config`, corrections must
be named explicitly. Four tests now pin the behaviour.

**Lesson.** cwd-relative defaults are ambient state. Note the test suite caught this by accident,
not by design — nothing was testing discovery. That is why the regression test went in.

---

### A test that was wrong, correctly diagnosed for once

**Category:** process — a small win worth recording

The privacy test (a pinned photo near home must still be excluded) failed on first run. The
previous instinct would have been to suspect the override logic that had just been written.
`should_exclude_from_export` reads `media.is_near_home`, which is written by `HomeFilterStage` —
which the test never ran. Test bug, not code bug, and checking the predicate before the new code
took under a minute.

**Lesson.** The retro that keeps recurring here is "tests that were wrong rather than code". When
a brand-new feature's test fails, the new code is the *likely* culprit but not the automatic one —
reading the assertion's dependencies first is cheap.

## Encoded as project rules

- `overrides.toml` addresses everything by filename; cluster and event ids are never referenced,
  because they are reassigned on every run.
- Unresolvable and ambiguous override names are fatal, never skipped.
- A pin overrides the quality floor and the content-class filter, but **not** home exclusion —
  the privacy guarantee is not a quality judgement, and there is a test asserting a pinned photo
  near home stays out.
- Implicit config-adjacent file discovery, never cwd-relative.
- The plan doc now carries the measurement that justifies the override file, so nobody later
  reads the pin list as a workaround for an untuned threshold.
