# P01 — profiling real trip data (2026-07-26)

Ran the T17 profiler against a real 286-item / 1.9 GB export for the first time. It found two
bugs in my own code and one bad heuristic, and produced two binding amendments to the plan doc.

This is the entry that most justifies the Phase 0 step existing. Every problem below was
invisible against synthetic fixtures and obvious against real data.

---

### Video timestamps were the export time, silently, for every clip

**Cycle:** P01
**Cost:** would have corrupted day assignment for all video, indefinitely
**Category:** wrong-assumption

**Symptom.** The first real profile reported a **10-day span with 5 dates** for what is a 4-day
trip, a **5.88-day largest gap**, and 9 videos all timestamped within 2 minutes of each other on
the day I ran the export.

**Root cause.** I read `CreateDate`, falling back to `MediaCreateDate`. On Photos-exported
`.mov`, those fields — and every `Track*CreateDate` — hold the **export** time. The real capture
time lives only in `QuickTime:Keys:CreationDate`, which additionally carries the original UTC
offset. Confirmed with `exiftool -time:all -G1`: 20 date fields all reading `2026:07:26 18:43:20`
(export), and exactly one reading the true capture time, 8 days earlier, with its offset.

The suspicious part is that nothing looked broken. A plausible date in a plausible format, and
the 9 clips even clustered together consistently — they just clustered on the wrong day.

**Fix.** Field priority now differs by media kind, `Keys:CreationDate` first for video, and the
source field is recorded so a fallback to `CreateDate` on a video can be warned about. Plan doc
Module 2 amended; binding on T11 and T15. After the fix: 4-day span, 4 dates, largest gap 0.49
days.

**Lesson.** **For video, "creation date" is ambiguous between capture and export, and the
conventional field is the wrong one.** More generally: when several metadata fields could answer
a question, dump *all* of them on a real file once (`exiftool -time:all -G1 -a -s`) before picking.
And treat "the numbers are plausible" as no evidence at all — this bug produced a self-consistent
wrong answer, which is the hardest kind to notice.

### `OffsetTimeOriginal` lies, and the plan trusted it first

**Cycle:** P01
**Cost:** none yet — caught before T12 was built, which is the point
**Category:** design-flaw (in the plan, not the code)

**Symptom.** 13 items carried an offset 9 hours away from the one their own GPS coordinates
imply, interleaved among 273 items whose offset matched. Nine hours wrong is enough to move a
photo to the adjacent day.

**Root cause.** The plan's timezone fallback order put `OffsetTimeOriginal` at step 1,
unconditionally, with GPS only as a fallback for when the tag is absent. That treats the tag as
ground truth. It isn't: an edited or re-exported photo can carry the *editing machine's* offset
rather than the camera's, and that machine sat in a zone 9 hours from the trip's.

**Fix.** Reversed the priority: the EXIF offset is used only when it agrees with the offset the
item's own GPS implies; on disagreement **GPS wins** and the conflict is counted and reported.
Implemented the detection in the profiler now (`_offset_conflicts`, using `timezonefinder` +
`zoneinfo`) so the signal is visible before T12 is written, and amended Module 2 of the plan.
Added an `offset_gps_conflict.jpg` fixture — synthetic coordinates tagged with a mismatched
offset — so T12 has a regression case waiting for it.

**Lesson.** **Prefer physical evidence over self-reported metadata when they disagree.** GPS
coordinates are a measurement; a timezone tag is an assertion by whatever software last touched
the file. Where a plan encodes a trust order between two sources, real data is the only way to
find out it's backwards — and finding out *before* building the stage cost nothing, versus
debugging wrong day boundaries later.

### A p90 basis for event gaps systematically over-splits

**Cycle:** P01
**Cost:** ~5 min, no bug shipped
**Category:** wrong-assumption

**Symptom.** The suggested `events.gap_minutes` came out at 30 (clamped up from a p90 of 15
minutes). That would cut a dense sightseeing hour into many one-photo events.

**Root cause.** I picked p90 as the basis for "the gap that separates events" without thinking
about the ratio. Event boundaries are *rare* relative to shots within an event: a few hundred
photos across a handful of days is maybe 15–25 boundaries out of 285 gaps, so boundaries live
around p92–p95, not p90. This library is shot in bursts — median gap between consecutive shots
is **1 minute** — which makes the low percentiles useless as a signal.

**Fix.** Basis changed to p95, with the reasoning recorded at the call site. Suggestion is now 45
minutes, against a guessed default of 90 — so the guess was wrong in the *other* direction, and
by 2×.

**Lesson.** When choosing a percentile as a threshold, estimate what fraction of samples the
event you're trying to isolate actually represents, and pick accordingly. Also: the default I'd
guessed (90 min) and my first data-driven suggestion (30 min) bracketed the reasonable answer
from both sides, which is a useful reminder that a number derived from data is not automatically
right — the *derivation* has to be right too.

---

### NOTE: the profiler's two signals must be read together

Not an error, but worth recording. After the fix the profile reports `offset changes: 2` **and**
`offset vs GPS: 7 conflicts`. Read alone, the first suggests the trip crossed timezones twice.
Read together with the second, the truth is clear: this trip never left `+02:00`, and the "2
changes" are the mis-tagged cluster entering and leaving. A single metric would have been
actively misleading; the pair is diagnostic.

Relatedly, the crossing counter now requires a **sustained run** of 3+ consecutive items at an
offset. Counting any A→B change turned 13 interleaved outliers into "14 crossings". The fixture
set was extended to 3 items per side of its timezone crossing to match — a 1-item-per-side
crossing is genuinely indistinguishable from a mis-tag, and a fixture that can't tell the two
apart can't test the distinction.
